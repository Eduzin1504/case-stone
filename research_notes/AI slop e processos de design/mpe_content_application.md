# Authentic, non-generic social content for Brazilian MPEs (MEI, lojistas) and anti-"AI slop" patterns for a content-generation agent

Context (from the brief, not externally sourced): agent is for Impulso Stone (Instituto Stone social program for micro entrepreneurs). Tone: close, welcoming, educational, no jargon. Internal data point: participation-inviting ad ("Empreendedor(a), queremos...") CTR 8.8% vs. generic motivational post ("Vender mais não é sorte") CTR 0.77%, roughly 11x. Treat this as the strongest, most specific evidence available, since it is the program's own audience.

## Q1. Evidence on authenticity and engagement (UGC/creator vs polished, real faces, specificity, lo-fi, platform ranking)

### Takeaway
Platform signals (Instagram ranking + Mosseri's 2026 statements) and consumer surveys point the same way: original, human-made, visibly imperfect content is favored, and recycled or obviously synthetic content is penalized in distribution and in trust. Hard causal data on lo-fi vs polished Reels is thin and mostly vendor-produced. Academic evidence is mixed: UGC is seen as more credible, but firm-generated content can drive loyalty more.

### Cited Findings
**Instagram/Meta ranking and originality**
- April 30, 2024: Instagram announced four changes: a new ranking input to help smaller creators, replacing reposts with the original in recommendations, labeling reposts with a link to the original creator, and removing aggregators from recommendations. Aggregator accounts regain eligibility only when they drop below 10 reposts in a 30-day period. Significantly modified content is not replaced. — [Instagram Creators: Recommendations and Originality](https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality); [TechCrunch 2024-04-30](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators)
- 2022 precedent: Mosseri said Instagram would build classifiers to estimate how likely a post is to be original, and admitted the company "can't know for sure." — [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/20/instagram-ranking-system-recommend-original-content/?tpcc=tcplustwitter)
- April 2026 update (single secondary source): originality enforcement extended beyond Reels to photos and carousels. Watermarks, minor crops or basic reposts are unlikely to count as "original." Mosseri told aggregators to remix and credit. — [PetaPixel 2026-04-30](https://petapixel.com/2026-04-30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/) (not confirmed on an official Instagram page during this research)
- Mosseri's year-end Threads essay (Jan 1, 2026), via secondary reporting: "authenticity is becoming infinitely reproducible". Raw, imperfect content works as a signal of human origin ("this is real because it's imperfect"). Instagram may move toward fingerprinting or labeling real media at capture rather than chasing fakes. He also conceded that AI will be able to fake imperfection too. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/instagram-chief-ai-is-so-ubiquitous-it-will-be-more-practical-to-fingerprint-real-media-than-fake-media-202620080.html); [Om Malik analysis](https://om.co/2026/01/01/what-is-instagrams-adam-mosseri-really-saying-in-his-year-end-memo/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/06/adam-mosseri-instagram-warning-generative-ai-deepfakes/); [Creative Bloq (critical take)](https://www.creativebloq.com/art/digital-art/instagrams-boss-admits-ai-slop-has-won-but-where-does-that-leave-creatives)

**Meta creative guidance (secondary summaries of Meta's Reels playbook)**
- Showing brand and main message within the first 5 seconds is reported to make a Reel 1.7x more likely to rank in the top 20% for purchase intent. Meta also recommends letting creators advocate "in their words", using real people (customers, employees), and sound-on design (reported >80% of Reels viewed with sound on). — [Hallam summary of Meta Reels playbook](https://hallam.agency/blog/insights-from-metas-reels-playbook-for-maximising-reach-and-engagement/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-reels-tips-for-marketers/806808/)

**Industry reports on lo-fi / UGC**
- Hootsuite Social Trends 2026 (survey of more than 6,500 marketers) frames the year's theme as "authenticity beats AI output." — [Hootsuite Social Trends](https://hootsuite.com/research/social-trends); [summary](https://www.thinkdigital.travel/research-directory/hootsuite-social-trends-2026)
- Hootsuite 2024 consumer research: 62% of consumers say they are less likely to engage with or trust content they know was created by AI. — [Hootsuite research](https://hootsuite.com/research)
- Dash Social: lo-fi Reels had higher engagement rate, views and reach than hi-fi Reels (the commonly repeated "+40% views, +30% reach" is secondhand, and sample size and date are unverified). — [Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/lo-fi-vs-hi-fi-video-performance); [Sprout Social on lo-fi](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/lo-fi-content/)
- HubSpot 2025 Global Social Media Trends: shoppers generally prefer authenticity over highly polished content. — [HubSpot report PDF](https://www.hubspot.com/hubfs/HubSpot%20x%20Masters%20in%20Marketing%20-%202025%20Global%20Social%20Media%20Trends%20Report.pdf)
- Emplifi Q3 2025 benchmarks: UGC-focused posts delivered about 2x the conversion-rate lift of Q2 (5.29x). — [Emplifi](https://emplifi.io/resources/blog/q2-2025-benchmarks/)
- Counterpoint: unpolished content only wins when it has a strategy. Production value is not the variable, and "content that looks like an ad gets treated like an ad." — [Breef](https://www.breef.com/breefingroom/articles/content-that-looks-like-an-ad-gets-treated-like-an-ad); [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/why-unpolished-reels-win-an-instagram-growth-strategy-for-business/)

**Academic**
- Meta-analysis (223 samples, N=97,709): for most attribute-to-loyalty relationships, firm-generated content had a *stronger* effect than UGC. Brand-made content is not inherently worse. — [Tyrväinen, Karjaluoto & Ukpabi 2023, J. Interactive Marketing](https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_87196)
- Consumers perceive content created by other consumers as more believable than marketing communications. — [JAMS article, doi 10.1007/s11747-023-00982-y](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11747-023-00982-y)

### Inferences
- The platform is technically pushing toward original, first-party footage. A small-business program's best asset is footage and voices of real empreendedores (with consent), not stock or recycled templates.
- "Authentic" means more than lo-fi aesthetics. The Impulso Stone CTR gap (8.8% vs 0.77%) is consistent with a broader pattern: content that addresses a specific person and asks them to *do* something beats abstract motivational claims. Specificity and participation probably matter more than production polish.
- Since firm-generated content can outperform UGC on loyalty, the best mix is probably brand-led *educational* content delivered by real people (instructors, participants), rather than pure UGC.

### Gaps
- No primary Later report found on lo-fi vs polished. Dash Social figures lack a verifiable methodology.
- No original Mosseri Threads text was read, so the quotes are secondhand.
- No peer-reviewed field study isolating "real face vs no face" on Instagram Reels for small businesses.

## Q2. Brazilian MPE context (channels, consumption, tone, trust drivers)

### Takeaway
Brazilian small businesses run on WhatsApp plus Instagram. Brazilian social users say they strongly value human-made content, even imperfect, and distrust AI-generated images. Sebrae's own guidance stresses informal language, bastidores (behind the scenes), and useful content before selling.

### Cited Findings (Brazil-specific)
- Sebrae Pulso dos Pequenos Negócios, 9th ed. (fieldwork Nov 25 to Dec 17, 2024): channels used were WhatsApp 81%, Instagram 60% (now ahead of Facebook), and Facebook 34% (down from 40%). About 70% sell through at least one digital tool. 48% have paid for online ads (59% of MPEs vs 39% of MEIs). Sample size was not disclosed in the article. — [Agência Sebrae, 2025-02-17](https://agenciasebrae.com.br/dados/quase-metade-dos-pequenos-negocios-investe-em-propaganda-paga-na-internet)
- Sebrae "Transformação Digital nos Pequenos Negócios" (reported Jan 2024): 7 in 10 small businesses have social profiles, with Instagram at 64% and Facebook at 41%. — [Agência Sebrae](https://agenciasebrae.com.br/dados/sete-em-cada-10-pequenos-negocios-tem-perfis-nas-redes-sociais-instagram-e-a-plataforma-preferida); [Mercado & Consumo](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/05/01/2024/ecommerce/sete-em-cada-10-pequenos-negocios-brasileiros-possuem-perfis-nas-redes-sociais/)
- A later Pulso edition (12th, 2026) reports WhatsApp at 82% of MEIs/MPEs. This comes from a search snippet and was not verified in the primary source. — [Agência Sebrae](https://agenciasebrae.com.br/dados/pequenos-negocios-ampliam-presenca-digital-para-conquistar-clientes)
- Also on the same Sebrae page, from separate studies: 8 in 10 small businesses use or plan to use AI for marketing and communication, and 2 in 3 that hire influencers report positive results. — [Agência Sebrae](https://agenciasebrae.com.br/dados/quase-metade-dos-pequenos-negocios-investe-em-propaganda-paga-na-internet)
- Influency.me + Opinion Box, "Consumo e Influência Digital 2026" (n=1,201 Brazilian social users): 84% value material made by people *even with imperfections*. 54% do not approve of artificially generated images, 43% say AI in product content creates doubt, and 40% see AI as a useful tool. — [Estado de Minas, 2026-06-15](https://www.em.com.br/mundo-corporativo/2026/06/7441014-consumidores-desconfiam-de-imagens-geradas-por-ia.html)
- The same report series says smaller profiles perceived as specialists can generate higher trust than big creators. The 2025 edition (n=2,000, ±2.2pp) says excess advertising reduces engagement. — [Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/midia/o-que-determina-a-relevancia-na-creator-economy); [Nosso Meio](https://nossomeio.com.br/estudo-revela-que-72-dos-brasileiros-escolhem-influenciadores-pelos-temas-que-abordam-apenas-6-escolhem-por-aparencia-fisica/) (72% choose influencers by topics covered, 6% by appearance)
- Counterpoint: Brain Inteligência Estratégica (Sep 2025, n=1,003) found Brazilians trust price more than influencers. — [Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/marketing/brasileiros-confiam-mais-em-preco-do-que-em-influenciadores)
- Kantar (2023): only 21% of consumers fully trust content received via online channels, and influencers help overcome digital distrust. — [Kantar Brasil](https://www.kantar.com/brazil/Inspiration/Marcas/O-poder-dos-influenciadores-na-hora-de-superar-a-desconfianca-digital)
- IAB Brasil #Publi 2025: trust, authenticity and creativity are key, alongside clear ad identification. — reported in search results; primary PDF not opened ([Opinion Box/Influency PDFs](https://content.app-us1.com/JY8yY/2025/09/11/076136f7-a312-48f2-a5b6-d51384054bf4.pdf))
- Sebrae guidance on tone and content: define a brand persona, tone and language matched to the audience. Social media is "um ambiente menos formal". Many MEIs use Instagram only as a vitrine when they should show bastidores, rotina, histórias, processos. Share learning to build connection. Give useful, educational value before selling. Post simple content consistently. Avoid polarizing political and religious topics. — [Sebrae e-book Marketing de Conteúdo](https://sebrae.com.br/Sebrae/Portal%20Sebrae/Arquivos/07_148%20E-BOOK%20Marketing%20de%20Conte%C3%BAdo.pdf); [Sebrae SP 10 dicas](https://sebrae.com.br/Sebrae/Portal%20Sebrae/UFs/SP/Programas%20e%20Solu%C3%A7%C3%B5es/Kit%20Enfrentamento/10_DICAS_PARA_SEU_NEG%C3%93CIO_BOMBAR_NAS_REDES_SOCIAIS.pdf); [Agência Sebrae on crises/brand](https://agenciasebrae.com.br/cultura-empreendedora/como-pequenos-negocios-podem-evitar-crises-e-potencializar-sua-marca-nas-redes-sociais); [Sebrae PE creator guide](https://sebrae.com.br/Sebrae/Portal%20Sebrae/UFs/PE/Anexos/Guia%20completo%20de%20como%20ser%20um%20criador%20de%20conteudos.pdf)

### Inferences
- WhatsApp is the conversion channel and Instagram is discovery. CTAs that hand off to WhatsApp ("me chama no zap", "manda 'QUERO' no direct") likely fit MPE behavior better than link-in-bio CTAs. This should be tested.
- Since 84% value human-made content and 54% reject AI images, the agent should generate *text and scripts* for real people to record. It should not generate synthetic people or "photos" of empreendedores.
- "Small specialist profile" trust suggests featuring peer empreendedores and Impulso instructors as the faces of the content, rather than a corporate voice.
- Concrete tone rules from this evidence: use "você" and direct address ("Empreendedor(a), ..."). Use the audience's own vocabulary (maquininha, fiado, capital de giro explained simply, "fechar o mês", "pix"). Name a concrete situation (day of week, sector, value in R$).

### Gaps
- No rigorous source found on regionalisms' effect on engagement (e.g., Nordeste vs Sudeste vocabulary). This is a real evidence gap, so A/B tests by region are recommended.
- Sebrae Pulso sample sizes and methodology were not available in the articles.
- No Sebrae data on *which content formats* (Reels vs carousel) drive MPE sales.

## Q3. Methods to keep AI-generated marketing copy specific and non-generic

### Takeaway
LLMs converge on the same "safe" ideas within a model and across models, and their own creativity prompts barely help. The documented fixes are: ground generation in retrieved real examples and data; ban known slop patterns mechanically; generate many diverse candidates (e.g., Verbalized Sampling) and then select; and evaluate with blinded, decomposed rubrics plus human review. Grounding in real performance data (CTR) has produced measured gains in production ad systems.

### Cited Findings
**The problem (homogenization / mode collapse)**
- GPT-4 story ideas made individual stories rated more creative, especially for less creative writers, but the stories were more similar to each other. Individual gain comes at the cost of collective diversity. — [Doshi & Hauser 2024, Science Advances](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532)
- LLM responses to creative prompts are much more similar to *each other* (across different models) than human responses are. System prompts encouraging creativity only slightly increase variability. — [Wenger & Kenett, arXiv:2501.19361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19361v1)
- Model-level convergence ("Artificial Hivemind"): text is stylistically smooth but structurally repetitive. — [arXiv:2604.01504](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01504)
- N-gram novelty alone is a weak creativity metric, because high novelty correlates with lower pragmatic sense. — [arXiv:2509.22641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22641)
- Homogenization should be evaluated per task, using a taxonomy-guided framework. — [arXiv:2509.21267](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21267)

**Diversity techniques**
- Verbalized Sampling (VS): a training-free prompt that asks for N candidates *with their probabilities* (e.g., "Generate 5 [hooks] and their corresponding probabilities"), optionally sampling from the tails. The authors attribute mode collapse to typicality bias in preference data, and report 1.6-2.1x diversity gains in creative writing, larger for more capable models (author-reported). Accepted at ICML 2026. Code: CHATS-lab/verbalize-sampling. — [arXiv:2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171v3); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/zhang26hy.html)

**Banned-phrase / slop suppression**
- Antislop (ICLR 2026): some slop patterns appear more than 1,000x more often in LLM output than in human text. The Antislop Sampler backtracks and resamples when a banned word, phrase or regex appears, and handles 8,000+ patterns (simple token banning breaks at around 2,000). An automated pipeline builds a *model-specific* slop profile against a human baseline. FTPO fine-tuning cuts slop by about 90% without hurting benchmarks, while DPO hurt writing quality and lexical diversity. MIT-licensed. — [arXiv:2510.15061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061); [ICLR poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008156); [GitHub antislop-sampler](https://github.com/sam-paech/antislop-sampler)

**Retrieval-grounded ad copy with measured outcomes**
- MarketingFM (June 2025): retrieval-augmented, keyword-specific ad copy beat templates in an online test, with up to 9% higher CTR, 12% more impressions and 0.38% lower CPC. The automated evaluator (AutoEval-Main) reached 89.57% agreement with human reviewers. — [arXiv:2506.17863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17863v1)
- CTR-driven ad text generation (July 2025): uses retrieved exemplars plus reasoning to sample diverse ad texts, then CTR-weighted preference optimization, with significant CTR gains on a large shopping platform. The authors note that LLM ad text does *not* inherently beat human-written text on CTR. — [arXiv:2507.20227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20227v3)
- Fine-tuned copy framework with expert ratings on novelty and fluency, and online A/B results (+12.5% CTR, +8.3% CVR). PDF extraction was messy, so verify these figures. — [arXiv:2505.23809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23809v1)

**LLM-as-judge caveats**
- LLM judges rate their own creative outputs as more creative. Mitigations reduce this bias but do not eliminate it. — [Tabatabaeian, Moon & Johnson (CogSci)](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6nk8f4xn/qt6nk8f4xn.pdf)
- Under blind evaluation (no self/other labels), self-preference largely disappears, and labels alone can induce bias. — [EMNLP 2026 Findings](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1571.pdf)
- Much of the measured self-preference reflects evaluator uncertainty (89.6% in their setup). — [Roytburg et al., arXiv:2601.22548](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22548)
- Structured multi-dimensional (decomposed) evaluation cut self-preference by 31.5% on average. Long chain-of-thought before judging also reduces harmful self-preference. — [arXiv:2604.22891](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22891); [arXiv:2504.03846](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03846v3)
- LLM creativity scores differed significantly from human scores across all 11 dimensions tested (snippet only). — [arXiv:2608.23705](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.23705)

### Inferences (concrete pipeline for the Impulso Stone agent)
1. **Grounding corpus (RAG)**: index Impulso Stone's own past posts and ads, labeled with CTR, saves and comments. Retrieve top performers *and* worst performers as contrastive examples (e.g., "Empreendedor(a), queremos..." at 8.8% vs "Vender mais não é sorte" at 0.77%). Also index real participant stories, FAQs from WhatsApp and DMs, and real comment language, so the agent borrows the audience's words rather than inventing them.
2. **Specificity slots as hard constraints**: every brief must fill {segmento (e.g., salão, marmita, loja de roupa), cidade/região, dor concreta, número em R$ or time, pessoa real/depoimento, data/ocasião (Dia das Mães, 5º dia útil)}. Reject generation if slots are empty.
3. **Brand voice guide with do/don't pairs**: e.g., DO "Você que vende marmita: quanto sobra no fim do mês?" / DON'T "Transforme seu negócio e conquiste o sucesso!". Use participation CTAs (perguntar, votar, contar sua história, responder no direct) over motivational slogans.
4. **Banned-phrase list (PT-BR) enforced in code, not just prompt**: "alavancar", "transforme seu negócio", "o sucesso não é sorte", "no mundo atual", "descubra o poder", "jornada empreendedora", "potencialize", "desbloqueie", "não é só X, é Y", and emoji or em-dash pileups. Build a model-specific list Antislop-style by diffing agent outputs against a corpus of real MEI posts and comments. Regex check post-generation, then regenerate.
5. **Diverse candidate generation**: use a VS-style prompt that asks for 8-10 hooks across *distinct angles* (pergunta, número, bastidor, erro comum, depoimento, convite) with probabilities, and keep some low-probability ones. Enforce an angle taxonomy so candidates aren't paraphrases.
6. **Blinded, decomposed judge**: score each candidate separately (no "this is ours" labels) on specificity (names a concrete person, sector or number?), audience voice match, clear single action, jargon-free, and "could any brand have posted this?" (genericness test: swap the brand name; if it still works, fail). Use a different model family from the generator when possible.
7. **Human-in-the-loop**: a social team and ideally 2-3 real empreendedores pick from a shortlist. Feed published results back into the RAG corpus (closing the loop like the CTR-weighted paper).
8. **Measure diversity at the batch level**, not just quality per post (Doshi & Hauser's collective-diversity risk): track embedding similarity across a month's posts and flag drift toward sameness.

### Gaps
- No study found specifically on PT-BR marketing slop patterns. The banned list above is a practitioner inference and should be validated empirically.
- No published evaluation of Verbalized Sampling on ad copy or short social hooks specifically.
- Automated judge agreement with humans (89.57%) comes from e-commerce keyword ads and may not transfer to educational social posts.

## Q4. Disclosure, platform policy and audience reactions to AI ads

### Takeaway
Meta auto-labels ads ("AI info") made with its own generative tools, and detects third-party AI via C2PA and similar signals. Disclosure is mandatory for political and social-issue ads, which can cover a social program's ads. Audience research is mixed but leans negative: AI ads are rated less engaging and creative, and labels can lower trust. Clear, specific disclosure of *how* AI was used mitigates the effect. Brazilian users specifically distrust AI images.

### Cited Findings
- Meta labels ad images and videos created or significantly edited with its gen-AI tools as "AI info". The label goes in the three-dot menu or next to "Sponsored", and directly next to "Sponsored" if a photorealistic human was generated. Third-party AI is detected via industry signals (C2PA) and shown in "About this ad". Minor edits (resizing, color correction) don't trigger the label. Label names vary by region. Updated June 1, 2026. — [Meta Newsroom, Expanding GenAI Transparency for Ads](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/); [Meta Help: AI images in ads](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/355108217670024/); [Meta Help: identify AI content](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/)
- Advertisers running political or social-issue ads must disclose AI-created or edited image, video or audio. — [Meta Help](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/355108217670024/)
- Koning & Voorveld 2025 (J. Interactive Advertising, N=304): AI disclosures increased persuasion knowledge and *decreased* trust in both the ad and the organization. — [UvA](https://dare.uva.nl/id/dc6a541b-3dac-4913-9f07-a6573b5764f0)
- Le et al. 2026 (Psychology & Marketing): transparency about the *role* AI played (not just "it's AI") improves process credibility and ad attitudes. The effect is stronger when framed as compliance or regulatory. — [White Rose eprint](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/241702/3/Psychology%20and%20Marketing%20-%202026%20-%20Le%20-%20More%20Than%20Saying%20%20It%20s%20AI%20%20%20How%20Role%20Disclosure%20Transparency%20in%20AI%E2%80%90Generated%20Ads.pdf)
- Several master's theses (2025) found no direct effect of disclosure on purchase intent or brand trust (weak evidence). — [Erasmus thesis](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/76583/); [Gothenburg](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/server/api/core/bitstreams/2207117d-b5ac-4be2-b0c3-98071825c5b4/content)
- NIQ (2024, presented at CES 2025): AI video ads were rated more "annoying, boring, confusing", and even high-quality ones produced weaker memory activation. NIQ warns of a negative halo on the brand. — [NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2024/niq-research-uncovers-hidden-consumer-attitudes-toward-ai-generated-ads/?tp=1)
- Ipsos + Syracuse (2026): only 13% could confidently identify an AI ad (same rate as false positives on human ads), but in side-by-side comparison AI ads were rated less creative and less emotionally engaging. — [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/ai-ads-are-good-enough-and-thats-problem)
- IAB + Sonata: 45% of Gen Z/Millennials feel positive about AI ads vs 82% of ad execs who believed they did. Coca-Cola's 2024 and 2025 AI holiday ads drew backlash read as cost-cutting and inauthentic. — [MarTech](https://martech.org/why-ai-driven-creative-is-failing-and-how-to-fix-it/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/410723/)
- Brazil: 54% disapprove of AI-generated images and 43% say AI in product content creates doubt. — [Estado de Minas / Influency.me + Opinion Box 2026](https://www.em.com.br/mundo-corporativo/2026/06/7441014-consumidores-desconfiam-de-imagens-geradas-por-ia.html)

### Inferences
- Lowest-risk design: AI drafts *text* (scripts, captions, hooks). Real people record it with real footage. No synthetic faces of "empreendedores". This avoids the "AI info" label next to "Sponsored" and the Brazilian distrust of AI images.
- Because Impulso Stone is a social program, some ads may be classified as social-issue ads. Check whether Meta's mandatory AI disclosure applies before using any AI-generated visuals.
- If AI visuals are used, disclose the role specifically (e.g., "ilustração feita com IA; histórias são de empreendedores reais"), consistent with Le et al.

### Gaps
- No Brazil-specific experiment on AI-label effects on ad CTR or trust was found.
- Brazilian regulation (e.g., the PL 2338 AI bill, CONAR guidance on AI ads) was not researched here and should be checked separately.
- The June 2026 Meta update details were read only via search summary, so placement rules may have changed again.
